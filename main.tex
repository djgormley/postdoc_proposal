\section*{Research Proposal}

\subsection*{1. The science goal and the gap}

Redshifted 21\,cm intensity mapping measures neutral-hydrogen clustering over volumes and redshifts that galaxy surveys reach only sparsely, with instrumental systematics substantially different from optical spectroscopy. The prize is the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) feature, which fixes the expansion history through $H(z)\,r_d$ and $D_A(z)/r_d$, and the redshift-space anisotropy, which fixes the growth rate $f\sigma_8$. CHIME's 400--800\,MHz band is 21\,cm emission at $0.8<z<2.5$~\cite[Abstract; Sec.~I; Fig.~1]{chime2022}, overlapping DESI's quasar and Lyman-$\alpha$ samples~\cite[Sec.~2.2; Table~1]{desi2024} and Roman's planned $z=1$--2 H$\alpha$ spectroscopic survey~\cite[Abstract]{roman2021}; a BAO measurement there is a complementary dark-energy probe with largely independent instrumental systematics.

The field has reached an inflection point. Following CHIME's detections in cross-correlation with galaxy surveys~\cite[Abstract]{chime2023} and the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest~\cite[Abstract; Sec.~VI.2; Table~1]{chime2024lya}, the collaboration has measured the 21\,cm auto-power spectrum at $z\simeq1$: $12.4\sigma$ from 94 nights over 608--708\,MHz at $0.4\lesssim k\lesssim1.5\hMpc$, with a new pipeline~\cite[Abstract; Table~2]{chime2025auto}. It is the first CHIME 21\,cm large-scale-structure measurement at this redshift that does not use an external catalog, and it constrains the combined HI-amplitude parameter $\mathcal A_{\rm HI}$ together with phenomenological small-scale shape terms~\cite[Secs.~6.2.1 and 6.2.3; Eqs.~(74)--(75), (78)]{chime2025auto}.

It does not, and cannot yet, constrain the BAO scale. The pipeline uses the DAYENU inverse-covariance high-pass filter~\cite[Sec.~3.2; Eqs.~(21)--(25)]{dayenu} with a CHIME-specific cutoff at 200\,ns~\cite[Sec.~3.2.3; Eqs.~(15)--(18)]{chime2025auto}, and then discards every line-of-sight mode below 280\,ns, where the filter response has not recovered, so the measurement begins at $\kpar=0.35\hMpc$~\cite[Sec.~5.3; Eq.~(64); Fig.~10]{chime2025auto}. Every mode with $|k|<0.35\hMpc$ has $\kpar<0.35$, so the BAO wiggles at $k\simeq0.05$--$0.3\hMpc$ (delays of roughly 40--240\,ns at $z=1.16$; Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}) are removed in their entirety. The collaboration says so plainly: the filter ``comes at the cost of discarding the low-$\kpar$ modes that are most sensitive to cosmological parameters''~\cite[Sec.~3.2]{chime2025auto}. The auto-spectrum detection is the amplitude; the cosmology is in the window that was thrown away.

\textbf{What fills that window is the question this proposal turns on, and its first task is to answer it.} The collaboration expects foregrounds in the beamformed data to be confined below roughly 200\,ns once instrument chromaticity is included; it attributes that chromaticity to mutual coupling and to reflections in the analog chain that ``introduce sinusoidal ripples in the bandpass'', bounded by ``the physical layout and electrical length scales of the telescope''~\cite[Sec.~3.2; Sec.~3.2.3, Eqs.~(15)--(18)]{chime2025auto}, and it subtracts a daily estimate of the additive cross-talk~\cite[Sec.~3.5.2; Eq.~(27)]{chime2025auto}. The named contributors are discrete delays: a 30\,MHz ripple from a standing wave in the $\sim$5\,m feed--reflector cavity, i.e.\ 33\,ns, whose peaks shift with declination~\cite[Sec.~V; Figs.~4--5]{chime2024holo} and which the daily Cygnus~A calibration imprints on all data~\cite[Fig.~5]{chime2025auto}; cross-talk delays plausibly extending to the 78\,m instrumented cylinder length, a one-way light time of $\sim$260\,ns~\cite[Appendix; Table~2]{chime2022}; and round-trip cable reflections, with a 50\,m, 390\,ns path documented in the current analysis~\cite[Sec.~7.9]{chime2025auto}. What has not been established is how much of the power in the 100--280\,ns window sits at those discrete delays and how much is smooth chromaticity or geometric wedge. That is the hypothesis this proposal tests: \emph{discrete delays dominate the reclaimable part of the window}. It is testable on existing data with the statistic of Section~3, and testing it is the first decision gate of the work plan (Section~5).

If the hypothesis holds, the program has three steps. \emph{Detect:} a blind census of every discrete delay in CHIME, with the foregrounds as the illuminating source. \emph{Estimate and subtract:} a few-parameter model of each delay, per declination and per transit, removed by a delay-shift canceller. \emph{Certify:} calibrated false-alarm rates, split-sample estimation so the correction cannot subtract the signal it was fit on, and a residual budget priced in BAO parameter bias --- so that the foreground cut is lowered by a certified amount rather than a hoped-for one. The toolkit is cyclostationary signal processing (CSP), which I have used for fifteen years on communications signals and NASA spectrum sensors; my dissertation on CHIME baseband supplies the pipelines to deploy it at scale.

The requirement is a number (Fig.~\ref{fig:taucut}): a Fisher forecast built on the collaboration's published survey and instrument parameters shows that CHIME's seven-year archive yields a $5\sigma$ BAO detection if the certified cut reaches $\tau_{\rm cut}\simeq100$--$140$\,ns depending on sky area ($\simeq150$\,ns if the filter's soft transition is credited), $3\sigma$ at $\simeq110$--$150$\,ns, and essentially nothing at the present 280\,ns. That forecast is conditional on the archive and the sky area, which are the collaboration's plan rather than this proposal's deliverable; Section~4 separates the dependencies.

\subsection*{2. The physical model, and what the pipeline already does}

For one polarization, east--west baseline and declination bin, write the beamformed visibility at one 9.94\,s integration as
\begin{equation}
d(\nu) \;=\; \bar g(\nu)\Big[1 + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \epsilon_k\, e^{\,2\pi i \nu \tau_k}\Big]\, s(\nu) \;+\; \sum_{j} c_j\, e^{\,2\pi i \nu \tau'_j} \;+\; n(\nu),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $s(\nu)$ is the sky (foregrounds $F$ plus the 21\,cm signal $h$, $F\sim10^4$--$10^5\,h$), $\bar g$ the smooth gain, $\{\tau_k,\epsilon_k\}$ the delays and complex amplitudes of multiplicative reflections, $\{\tau'_j,c_j\}$ the additive cross-talk paths, and $n$ noise. In delay space,
\begin{equation}
D(\tau) \;=\; S(\tau) + \sum_k \epsilon_k\, S(\tau - \tau_k) + \sum_j c_j\, \delta(\tau-\tau'_j) + N(\tau).
\label{eq:delay}
\end{equation}
The foreground occupies $|\tau|\lesssim\tau_F$, where $\tau_F$ depends on baseline and direction: a few tens of ns for main-lobe emission on short baselines, up to the horizon delay $b/c\simeq220$\,ns for far-sidelobe sources on the 66\,m baselines. A reflection does not smear it; it \emph{copies} whatever foreground structure the baseline carries, attenuated by $|\epsilon_k|$, to $\tau_k$ --- a compact copy on short baselines, a broad one where the wedge is broad. A cross-talk path plants a spike. Both are localized in delay and periodic in frequency.

The pipeline already treats each term. DAYENU strongly attenuates modes below $\tau_{\rm cut}=200$\,ns, at the price described above. The additive cross-talk is estimated separately each sidereal day, polarization, channel, east--west baseline and north--south position from weighted medians of the real and imaginary visibilities over a quarter-specific one-hour local-ERA reference interval, and subtracted~\cite[Sec.~3.5.2; Eq.~(27)]{chime2025auto}; that removes the component stable in the reference interval and leaves its drift and any sky dependence, and it does nothing for the multiplicative terms, which track the sky. For residual leakage, the general HyFoReS cross-correlation estimator~\cite[Sec.~III.A; Eqs.~(5), (9), (11), pp.~3--4]{hyfores} is implemented as a coefficient $\delta g_{pfe}$ per polarization, channel and baseline by cross-correlating the high-pass data against a low-pass foreground template, averaged over the sky and estimated independently each day~\cite[Sec.~3.3; Eq.~(23)]{chime2025auto}. In simulation, HyFoReS reduces bias from $10^{-5}$--$10^{-4}$ bandpass errors mostly below thermal noise, while $10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$ errors can be suppressed by roughly three orders of magnitude; gain-estimation noise and second-order errors set the stated limits~\cite[Secs.~IV.A--IV.B; Figs.~2 and 4, pp.~8--11; Sec.~V.B, p.~14]{hyfores_sim}. On data the collaboration reports a modest reduction~\cite[Sec.~3.3; Eq.~(24); Fig.~3]{chime2025auto}. With one free coefficient per channel, HyFoReS averages over declination and time to control noise on $\sim$256 parameters; the dominant ripple's peaks shift with declination~\cite[Sec.~V; Fig.~5]{chime2024holo}, so a sky average can partially cancel what it fits if the corresponding phase varies. This proposal is a \emph{sparse-delay extension} of HyFoReS: $3K$ parameters, per declination and per transit, at the delays a census finds. The benchmark is head to head on the same data --- parameter count, signal loss, residual bias and compute --- and HyFoReS's authors are co-authors on the data I have requested.

Two pieces of context fix expectations. The 2023 stacking analysis used cuts of order 200\,ns that varied with polarization and declination~\cite[Sec.~IV.5; Eqs.~(53)--(57); Fig.~9; Sec.~V.1, Fig.~13]{chime2023}, comparable to the present one, but in cross-correlation with an external catalog, where residuals uncorrelated with the catalog average away; the auto-spectrum has no such immunity, which is why lowering the cut means removing leakage rather than tolerating it. And HERA's reflection fitting shares the physical picture but estimates narrow reflection delays and amplitudes from autocorrelation delay spectra~\cite[Sec.~3.1; Fig.~3, pp.~7--8]{kern2019} and applies the resulting antenna corrections to cross-correlations~\cite[Sec.~3; Fig.~5, pp.~5--8]{kern2020}, rather than estimating blindly from the coherence structure of the cross data.

\subsection*{3. Method: cyclostationarity along the frequency axis}

A process modulated by a periodic function is cyclostationary: its spectral correlation function, between components separated by a \emph{cycle frequency} $\alpha$, can be non-zero at cycle frequencies corresponding to periodic components, while stationary noise has none at $\alpha\ne0$~\cite[Sec.~3.2.1; Eqs.~(3.6)--(3.11), (3.19), pp.~643--645; Sec.~3.7, Eqs.~(3.88)--(3.95), pp.~651--652]{gardner2006}. More generally, second-order cyclostationary statistics can identify an unknown multipath channel without training data~\cite[Abstract, p.~340; Secs.~I.B--I.C; Eqs.~(1)--(2), p.~341]{tong1994}. My M.S.\ thesis implemented a low-memory version of this estimator in streaming form~\cite[Abstract, p.~vii; Sec.~3.2, pp.~27--29]{gormley2021thesis}; the FPGA spectrum sensor I built at NASA Glenn realized it in hardware~\cite[Sec.~II; Figs.~7--9, p.~3; Sec.~III, Table~II, pp.~3--4]{gormley2021}, and a later paper developed the hybrid memory--throughput extension~\cite[pp.~34--36]{gormley2023}. My dissertation works at first order (the ATSC pilot is a periodic mean, its optimal detector a tone matched filter) and contributes the certification discipline that carries over here~\cite{gormley2026a}.

Equation~(\ref{eq:model}) is the same mathematics with the axes renamed: frequency $\nu$ plays the role of time, delay $\tau$ the role of frequency, and a reflection at $\tau_k$ is a periodic modulation with cycle ``frequency'' $\tau_k$. The statistic is the \emph{delay--offset correlation}
\begin{equation}
C(\tau, \Delta) \;=\; \big\langle D(\tau)\, D^{*}(\tau-\Delta) \big\rangle, \qquad
\gamma(\tau,\Delta) \;=\; \frac{C(\tau,\Delta)}{\sqrt{\langle |D(\tau)|^2\rangle\, \langle |D(\tau-\Delta)|^2\rangle}},
\label{eq:coh}
\end{equation}
with the average over the integrations of a transit at fixed declination: different sky, same instrument state. Substituting Eq.~(\ref{eq:delay}):
\begin{itemize}
\item For anything stationary along frequency --- the 21\,cm signal and thermal noise --- $C(\tau,\Delta\ne0)$ vanishes in expectation. In a finite band with tapers, flagged channels and a chromatic beam it does not vanish exactly; it leaves a smooth, non-localized floor, and the null calibration below measures that floor rather than assuming it away.
\item For a reflection, $C(\tau,\tau_k)=\epsilon_k\langle|S(\tau-\tau_k)|^2\rangle$: a \emph{ridge} at $\Delta=\tau_k$ spanning $\tau\in[\tau_k-\tau_F,\tau_k+\tau_F]$, on which $|\gamma|\simeq1$ wherever $|\epsilon_k|^2P_F>P_N$, whatever the size of $\epsilon_k$: the leaked copy is coherent with its source. That is the detection statistic, and it is what makes the census blind, up to the model selection handled below.
\item Smooth chromaticity produces coherence spread over $\Delta$ rather than localized at discrete values (Fig.~\ref{fig:coh}, bottom). The same map therefore separates what this method can remove from what it cannot.
\end{itemize}
Estimation follows from detection: on a ridge, $\hat\epsilon_k(y,t)=C(\tau,\tau_k)/\langle|S(\tau-\tau_k)|^2\rangle$, with $S$ from the low-delay, foreground-dominated data. Its signal-to-noise is set by $|\epsilon_k|^2P_F/P_N$, the leaked copy against thermal noise --- not by the foreground-to-signal ratio --- and is a quantity the census measures term by term. Where it suffices per transit there is no need to average over the sky, which is the freedom needed to follow the declination-dependent phase of the 33\,ns term and intraday drift. The correction is a \emph{delay-shift canceller}, the delay-domain analogue, under the frequency--delay axis mapping, of CSP's frequency-shift (FRESH) filter~\cite[Sec.~3.6; Eqs.~(3.71)--(3.72), p.~650; Sec.~7.2, Eqs.~(7.1), (7.6)--(7.9), pp.~661--662]{gardner2006}: subtract $\sum_k\hat\epsilon_k(y,t)\,S_{\rm lpf}(\tau-\tau_k)$, with $S_{\rm lpf}$ the low-pass template the pipeline already forms. Setting $K$ to the number of channels and dropping the physical prior recovers HyFoReS's per-channel form; this is HyFoReS with the answer's structure built in. Additive cross-talk is first-order cyclostationarity, a periodic mean, and its spikes at $\tau'_j$ come out of the same census.

\textbf{Model selection.} The delay dictionary is data-dependent, and that is handled rather than assumed away. Ridges are accepted at a threshold set on the null-calibrated coherence with a trials correction over the $(\tau,\Delta)$ grid. The sum and difference terms that copies of the same foreground produce at $\tau_j\pm\tau_k$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:coh}) are identified by their $\tau$ position and excluded. Off-grid delays are refined by interpolating the ridge peak, and a delay error small against the inverse bandwidth is absorbed into the phase of $\epsilon_k$. $K$ is the smallest dictionary that leaves no ridge above threshold; every retained term costs signal, so $K$ is reported with the result.

\textbf{Statistical control.} (i)~\emph{False alarms:} two nulls, because one is not enough. The even--odd difference of sidereal-day stacks cancels everything sky-locked --- the reflection copies along with the sky --- so it calibrates the thermal-noise floor of $|\gamma|$ and nothing else. The structured floor from finite bandwidth, tapers, flagged channels and the chromatic beam is calibrated by injecting a simulated sky through the collaboration's beam model with no reflections and mapping its coherence. Thresholds are set at fixed false-alarm probability from the combined null, the policy I use for pilot masking~\cite{gormley2026a}. (ii)~\emph{Signal loss:} estimation and application are split across odd and even sidereal days. The two halves share the same sky, so this does not make the correction independent of the signal; what it removes is noise bias, since the noise in $\hat\epsilon_k$ is independent of the noise in the datum it corrects. Signal loss is measured, not argued: the same simulated 21\,cm sky is injected into both halves, propagated through estimation and cancellation, and the transfer function reported, as the filter's is now~\cite[Sec.~3.2.3; Eqs.~(17)--(19); Sec.~5.3, Eq.~(37), Fig.~10]{chime2025auto}. Because $\hat\epsilon_k$ is formed on ridges where the foreground exceeds the signal by $10^4$--$10^5$, the signal-correlated part of $\hat\epsilon_k$ is of order $h/(F\sqrt{N})$ and the induced loss correspondingly small, but that is the expectation the injection tests. (iii)~\emph{Parameter bias:} residual leakage after correction is converted into a bias on the BAO dilation parameter with the \texttt{RFIsher} forecasting framework~\cite{gormley2026b}, and the cut is lowered only as far as the certified residual allows. The target is a certified $\tau_{\rm cut}$, not a hoped-for one.

\textbf{Where it runs.} On the hybrid beamformed visibilities at the native 9.94\,s cadence, per polarization and east--west baseline, alongside the DAYENU filter and before sidereal averaging~\cite[Sec.~2.3; Sec.~3.2.2, Eq.~(9); Sec.~3.2.3, Eqs.~(17)--(18)]{chime2025auto}. The computation is short delay transforms and cross-products per integration; the streaming-survey layer I wrote for the baseband archive (now in \texttt{datatrail}, detector in \texttt{pilot-proxy})~\cite{gormley2026a} runs the same pattern over petabyte inventories with checkpointing.

\subsection*{4. Feasibility and preliminary work}

\textbf{What the window is worth, and what the forecast assumes.} Figure~\ref{fig:taucut} gives $A/\sigma_A$, the BAO detection significance, against the delay cut, computed with \texttt{RFIsher}, my Fisher framework built on RadioFisher's intensity-mapping and BAO formalism~\cite[Sec.~2.4, Eq.~(8), p.~5; Sec.~4.1, Eqs.~(13)--(14), Fig.~6, pp.~10--11; Sec.~4.2, Eqs.~(15)--(17), p.~11]{radiofisher} and the pinned implementation and outputs~\cite[commit \texttt{c7fae17f}; \path{scripts/run_taucut_sweep.py}, lines~318--390; \path{out/taucut_sweep.csv}, lines~22--28]{rfisher_code}. The 2025-field case combines the published area, band and observing time (2,200\,deg$^2$, 608--708\,MHz, 385\,h)~\cite[Abstract; Eq.~(46); Fig.~4]{chime2025auto} with the CHIME Overview value $T_{\rm sys}=55$\,K~\cite[Appendix~A; Table~2; Eq.~(A9)]{chime2022}; the other cases use the seven-year archive on two sky areas. Table~\ref{tab:scen} is the honest version: only the lower cut is delivered by this proposal; nearly seven years of archived data and extension to the remaining, lower-frequency band are separate future efforts described by the collaboration~\cite[Sec.~2.4; Sec.~3.2.1; Sec.~8]{chime2025auto}; the sky area is the largest uncertainty and is bracketed by the two archive rows. The forecast applies a hard mask at $\kpar<1.4\,\tau_{\rm cut}$, adopting the empirical 280/200 transition ratio in the current analysis~\cite[Sec.~5.3; Eq.~(64); Fig.~10]{chime2025auto}; this is an explicit modelling convention, not a universal filter scaling. The figure's axis is the retained floor $\tau_{\rm min}=1.4\,\tau_{\rm cut}$, and every number in the text is $\tau_{\rm cut}$. The soft variant is the same forecast with the published filter response in place of the excision. Noise normalization was checked against the published $12.4\sigma$ detection; matching it needs weaker small-scale damping than RadioFisher's fiducial ($\sigma_{\rm NL}=4.9$ rather than 7\,Mpc), which would loosen every threshold by 10--20\,ns, so the fiducial values are quoted. Three refinements are pending, and they do not all move the same way: baseline- and redshift-dependent cuts in place of one global delay would raise the significance; nonzero residual contamination \todo{run and add} would lower it; and a short-baseline-only reopening, the outcome if the wedge sets the cut on long baselines, would lower it and is the case the gate decides. The requirement quoted is therefore the zero-residual, global-cut case, an optimistic bound. The window is also worth far more for the radial scale than the transverse: at $\tau_{\rm cut}=125$\,ns on the full sky, $\sigma(\alpha_\parallel)=1.3\%$ but $\sigma(\alpha_\perp)=6.1\%$, because the modes the cut removes are the low-$\mu$ modes that carry $D_A$. $H(z)\,r_d$ is the near-term product; $D_A(z)/r_d$ needs a lower cut. The curve is steep --- from 125 to 150\,ns the significance falls by $2.2\times$ --- so the Year~2 milestone is a threshold, not a direction; and enlarging the accepted sky is worth as much as lowering the cut, a second reason to model leakage around bright sources rather than mask it.

\textbf{The estimator, and the test it enables.} The simulation of Fig.~\ref{fig:coh} solves Eq.~(\ref{eq:model}) for a delay-limited foreground $10^4$ times the signal, white noise, and a visibility gain $g_ig_j^*$ carrying reflections at 33\,ns ($\epsilon=10^{-2}$) and 175\,ns ($3\times10^{-3}$), over 600 integrations at one declination. The reflections appear as conjugate ridge pairs at $\Delta=\pm33$ and $\pm175$\,ns with $|\gamma|>0.98$; the quantitative discriminator is the ridge contrast $\max_\tau|\gamma|/\mathrm{med}_\tau|\gamma|$, which reaches $\approx20$ at the reflection delays over a floor of $\approx3$, while smooth chromaticity of the same rms gives $\approx2.4$ everywhere with no peaks. Two practical results fall out: the ridge set is the \emph{difference set} of the delay dictionary, so ridges are identified by $(\tau,\Delta)$ position rather than $\Delta$ alone; and off-grid delays leak across the delay axis at $\sim1/\pi n$ without apodization, so a Blackman--Harris taper is required. Run on the 2019 hybrid beamformed visibilities, the same map is the first decision gate: ridges at discrete $\Delta$ mean the window is reclaimable by this method; a smear means it belongs to beam modelling and this method delivers the reflection budget only. \todo{Replace Fig.~\ref{fig:coh} with the real-data map when the requested 2019 data are in hand: recover the 33\,ns term, its phase against declination, detection significance and estimator variance, and the content of 110--300\,ns.}

\subsection*{5. Work plan}

\textbf{Year 1 --- gate, census, estimator.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Gate (months 1--3):} the coherence map on the 2019 hybrid beamformed visibilities at several declinations either side of Cygnus~A, with the null calibrated on the even--odd difference. Ridges at discrete delays confirm the hypothesis of Section~1 and fix the dictionary; a smear redirects Year~2 to the reflection budget and to a hybrid with the collaboration's beam model.
\item \emph{Census:} two data products, because the archived visibilities average over redundant baseline copies and do not retain per-feed indexing. Per feed, the census runs on the daily per-input gain solutions from the Cygnus~A calibration --- 2048 inputs by 1024 channels per day, small and already archived --- whose delay transforms carry each feed's reflections directly. Per baseline, it runs the delay--offset coherence on the redundancy-averaged visibilities, per polarization, east--west baseline and declination, over the seven-year archive. Output: every discrete delay in CHIME with amplitude and phase against time, temperature and calibration epoch; the 33\,ns standing wave is the validation case and the 110--300\,ns population the priority. The coherence statistic costs $\sim$$10^6$ operations per integration and channel set, so the work is I/O-bound \todo{archive volume of the requested hybrid product, storage, and the named allocation}. The instrument analogue of my dissertation's transmitter census, and a paper on its own.
\item \emph{Estimator:} the delay-shift canceller on the 2019 dataset, with split-sample estimation and application; certified false-alarm and signal-loss behavior; the corrected delay spectrum at 100--280\,ns consistent with noise plus injected signal, and no ridges after correction. \emph{Milestone:} the published $12.4\sigma$ reproduced with the correction on, unchanged --- the null test of the method's own safety.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Year 2, if the gate fails.} If the map shows a smear rather than ridges, Year~2 is not a hybrid with the beam model in name only. Its deliverables become: the census as a named line item in the collaboration's systematics budget, with the per-feed gain-solution delays as a calibration product; the canceller applied to whatever discrete population does exist, with its own certified $\tau_{\rm cut}$; and the smear itself, which is a per-declination, per-baseline measurement of the chromatic beam's delay structure and feeds the holography-based beam model~\cite[Sec.~VI.3; Eqs.~(16)--(17); Figs.~16--17; Sec.~VII]{chime2024holo}. The milestone is a per-baseline delay budget and a forecast of the cut it supports, from the same framework as Fig.~\ref{fig:taucut}.

\textbf{Year 2 --- open the window.} Lower $\tau_{\rm cut}$ on the full archive as far as the certified residual allows and measure the 21\,cm power spectrum in the newly opened $\kpar$ bins with the collaboration's even/odd cross-spectrum and jackknife suite. \emph{Deliverable:} the first 21\,cm auto-spectrum at $z\simeq1$ below $\kpar=0.35\hMpc$, with a systematics budget in which each discrete delay is a named line item. \emph{Threshold:} a certified $\tau_{\rm cut}\le125$\,ns on the full sky, or $\le110$\,ns if the present mask is retained, the $5\sigma$ requirement of Table~\ref{tab:scen}; a certified 150\,ns yields 2.5--4$\sigma$ on the full sky, a first BAO-window measurement rather than a detection, and is the stated fallback.

\textbf{Year 3 --- BAO and transfer.} BAO-window analysis of the archive: fit the dilation parameter with the forecast-calibrated residual budget and report a constraint, or a certified statement of what remains before one. Port the census and canceller to CHORD, whose 64-dish pathfinder is being commissioned now and whose 512-dish core is planned for completion at the end of 2027, with full-telescope commissioning through 2028~\cite[Abstract, p.~2; Sec.~11, p.~18; Fig.~9, p.~19]{chord2026}: contingent on that published schedule, use pathfinder data in Year~1 and the full array in Year~3, with a dish--feed cavity at a different delay but the same physics. Cross-instrument test on a KICP spectrometer with a reflection cavity \todo{SPT-SLIM or TIM, per host}, where etalon and filter-bank ripples are the same periodic-in-frequency structure.

\subsection*{6. Risks}

\emph{The hypothesis fails: the window is smooth chromaticity, not discrete delays.} That is what the gate is for; the method then delivers the reflection budget, and Year~2 becomes a hybrid with beam modelling rather than a replacement for it. \emph{The cut is set by the foreground wedge on long baselines.} The horizon delay of CHIME's longest east--west baseline (66\,m) is $\sim$220\,ns. Wedge contamination scales with baseline length and is not localized in $\Delta$, so the gate distinguishes it; if it dominates, the short baselines that carry the large-scale transverse modes remain reclaimable and the long ones do not, and the forecast for that case \todo{short-baseline-only RFIsher run} replaces Table~\ref{tab:scen}. \emph{The requirement is steep.} A 25\,ns shortfall against 125\,ns costs a factor of two in significance; the 150\,ns fallback is stated rather than discovered. \emph{Amplitudes drift faster than the estimation cadence.} Per-transit estimation is limited by $|\epsilon_k|^2P_F/P_N$ \todo{quantify from the coherence map}; terms too weak for a single transit are pooled over a day and their drift bounded from the day-to-day difference. \emph{Lowering the cut exposes the filter's own mode coupling.} Simulated end to end as in the published analysis; the cut moves only as far as the certified residual permits. \emph{Access after the move.} Membership renews on application and does not depend on the host \todo{confirm the renewal timeline and that the Canadian compute allocation travels with it}.

\subsection*{7. Broader significance}

The method is instrument-agnostic: any spectrometer with a reflection cavity, a cable or a coupled neighbour has discrete-delay structure, and every intensity-mapping experiment pays for it in the same currency of discarded low-$\kpar$ modes. Developing it on CHIME --- the only instrument with a 21\,cm auto-spectrum detection and a seven-year archive --- and carrying it to CHORD, whose design emphasizes precision beam control and stable instrumental response~\cite[Abstract, p.~2; Sec.~1, pp.~2--3; Sec.~2.2, p.~4]{chord2026}, and to mm-wave line intensity mapping, puts the tool in the hands of the next generation of experiments as a certification of their systematics budgets. \todo{NHFP version: two sentences on relevance to NASA Astrophysics --- an independent BAO/growth probe at $1<z<2$, Roman's spectroscopic range, with disjoint systematics; and the applicant's intention to bring the method to NASA instrument pipelines.} \todo{KICP version: two sentences on fit --- dark energy from an independent tracer; systematics methodology shared with SPT-SLIM/TIM.}

\section*{Previous and Current Research}

My path to 21\,cm cosmology runs through fifteen years of detecting weak periodic structure in hostile radio environments: as a U.S.\ Army signals analyst (2007--2012), where a detector's false-alarm rate is a practical matter; at NASA Goddard (2016--2017), building FPGA back-ends for the Wideband RFI Mitigation testbed and implementing real-time spectral-kurtosis RFI detection for spaceborne radiometry; and at NASA Glenn (2017--2022), where as cognitive-radio lead for the Cognitive Communications Project I held technical authority over real-time FPGA/SDR systems for the Near Space Network and contributed the core FPGA and DSP engineering to the SCaN Testbed quantum-key-distribution emulation recognized by NASA Headquarters.

The scientific thread through that period is cyclostationary signal processing. My M.S.\ thesis (Cleveland State, 2021) developed a low-memory streaming spectral correlation analyzer for blind detection of digitally modulated waveforms~\cite[Abstract, p.~vii; Sec.~3.2, pp.~27--29]{gormley2021thesis}; I implemented it on FPGA as a spectrum sensor for CubeSat radios~\cite[Sec.~II; Figs.~7--9, p.~3; Sec.~III, Table~II, pp.~3--4]{gormley2021} and extended it with a hybrid memory--throughput architecture~\cite[pp.~34--36]{gormley2023}, work carried out in regular collaboration with C.~M.~Spooner, whose formulation of cyclic cumulants and spectral correlation estimation underlies the methods proposed here. At AiRANACULUS (2022--2023) I designed statistical detection and machine-learning methods for wideband RF sensing and interference mitigation~\cite[Sec.~IV, Figs.~5--6, pp.~3--4; Sec.~VI, Figs.~9 and 11, pp.~5--6]{mody2023a} and contributed to two NASA space-network resilience efforts~\cite[Secs.~III--V; Figs.~3--9, pp.~2--5]{mody2023b}; related NASA cognitive-network work used the streaming spectrum sensor and RF-feature-enhanced architectures~\cite[Sec.~II.F, p.~272; Sec.~V, pp.~278--279]{dudukovich2022}.

\textbf{Dissertation: The Pilot-Proxy Method (WVU, defense spring 2027; advisor K.~Bandura).} The CHIME analysis explicitly targets and masks broadcast-television contamination~\cite[Sec.~3.1.2; Appendix~A.1, Eqs.~(A12)--(A15); Table~1]{chime2025auto}. Every ATSC signal carries a pilot carrier at a fixed offset, so the pilot is a proxy for the whole 6\,MHz channel; it is a tone, first-order periodicity, so its optimal detector is a matched filter ($F$-statistic) rather than a spectral-correlation statistic. I implemented that detector as a bit-reproducible fixed-point GPU pipeline and ran it as a streaming survey over the baseband archive with per-epoch calibration measured from the data~\cite{gormley2026a}; the survey layer is now part of the \texttt{datatrail} tooling. Three results matter here. First, the masking-scaling argument: in a sidereal stack, sky signal and constant terrestrial interference are both coherent, so unmasked channels saturate at an interference bias floor and never reach BAO depth, while masking restores noise-limited $1/\sqrt{\eta T}$ scaling at a $1/\eta$ cost in wall-clock time; a ``canary'' bound on sub-threshold leakage certifies the claim. Second, thresholds set by the science requirement rather than the detection floor: per-channel null-bulk calibration at fixed false-alarm probability, priced in integration-time cost and BAO parameter bias by \texttt{RFIsher}~\cite{gormley2026b}. Third, a feed-by-feed census of DTV transmitters around the observatory. \todo{Add three headline numbers: detection depth relative to the noise floor, false-alarm rate achieved, and archive volume surveyed.} The detector is being integrated into the \texttt{kotekan} framework shared by CHIME and CHORD~\cite{gormley2026c}.

\textbf{Leadership and community.} At NASA Glenn I led the Cognitive Communications team and mentored interns and new-hire engineers; I have been session chair and reviewer for IEEE CCAAW, reviewer for IEEE MILCOM, and a NASA SBIR proposal reviewer. I have taught as a TA in circuit theory, digital signal processing, software-defined radio and senior capstone, taken part in NASA public outreach, and at WVU I help manage the DSPIRA program, which teaches signal processing through radio astronomy, in a programmatic rather than classroom role.

The proposal above is the natural next step: the second-order estimator from my M.S.\ and NASA work, the certification discipline and pipelines from my dissertation, pointed at the instrument instead of at the interference.
